Introduction

Executing an entire research workflow does not necessarily mean controlling it. Humans may still define the goals, the boundaries of allowable change, and the criteria for success.

This survey follows the transfer of three discovery decisions: search direction, search space, and evaluation signal. It connects that control-centered view to four families of AI artifacts.

ABSTRACT

Page 1

Artificial intelligence (AI) is reshaping how AI itself is developed. From designing learning algorithms to automating research workflows and evaluation, this expanding role has fueled the rapid growth of AI for AI (AI4AI). Yet a fundamental question remains: as AI takes on more research tasks, which decisions does it actually control? Even systems that execute entire research workflows may still rely on humans to define research goals, allowable modifications, and criteria for success. Existing taxonomies, typically organized by technique or target artifact, offer limited insight into these differences in research responsibility. This survey presents a unified, control-centered perspective on AI4AI, viewing its evolution as a progressive transfer of control over three key decisions in the discovery loop: search direction, search space, and evaluation signal. We introduce an L0–L3 control-source framework, complemented by full responsibility profiles, to characterize different forms of human–AI research responsibility without conflating control with overall capability or end-to-end autonomy. This perspective unifies research across AI4AI Learning, Inference, Data, and Infrastructure, while motivating an evaluation framework that goes beyond downstream performance to assess decision quality, discovery efficiency, transfer, evaluator independence, and robustness under optimization. Looking further ahead, AI4AI may enable recursive self-improvement, where advances in AI enhance the process of making subsequent discoveries. If sustained and independently validated, such improvements could offer a pathway toward superintelligence. By connecting research autonomy with the conditions for reliable and cumulative progress, this survey provides a unified foundation for understanding the evolution and long-term potential of AI4AI.

01

When is research control worth delegating?

AI-provided direction, search space, or evaluation does not necessarily improve research quality. The value of delegation may depend on how quickly errors become observable, whether they are reversible, how costly independent verification is, and how much human effort the decision would otherwise require. Should research autonomy be a fixed configuration or a revisable allocation of authority that changes with evidence and risk?

FUTURE WORK · CAUSAL EVALUATION AND ADAPTIVE ALLOCATION OF CONTROL

Expose D, S, and E as inspectable, versioned components that can be replaced independently or jointly, record human effort and rescue interventions, and test delegation policies that allocate authority by uncertainty, consequence, and verification cost.

Section 8.1 · page 49
02

Can AI expand the boundaries of practical discoverability?

A sufficiently expressive language may already represent a desirable algorithm without making it discoverable within a realistic budget. The central question for search-space construction is whether AI can make previously inaccessible, high-quality solutions practically reachable, and whether it can improve how research itself is organized.

FUTURE WORK · OPEN-ENDED SEARCH-SPACE CONSTRUCTION AND META-DISCOVERY

Ask systems to diagnose why an existing representation obstructs progress and propose a space-defining artifact; count the cost of constructing the space and test it on unseen tasks with several compatible search procedures.

Section 8.1 · page 49
03

What establishes progress when evaluation itself evolves?

When a candidate system and its evaluator change together, rising internal scores no longer isolate capability gains from changes in measurement. A revised evaluator may catch overlooked failures, or it may reward easier tasks, favor the generator’s outputs, or relax acceptance conditions. Reliable assessment needs evidence that is not reducible to the loop’s own judgments.

FUTURE WORK · INDEPENDENTLY GROUNDED EVALUATOR CO-EVOLUTION

Pair an adaptive internal evaluator with a validation path the improving system cannot rewrite during the run; vary search intensity and evaluator flexibility while measuring false acceptance, false rejection, and divergence from external outcomes.

Section 8.1 · page 49
04

When do local improvements become cumulative research knowledge?

An artifact can be useful without revealing why it works or how it should inform later designs. The stronger question is whether AI4AI can turn experimental outcomes into knowledge that supports new predictions, such as executable abstractions, supported design rules, or validated negative results, and whether that contribution can be separated from more compute or longer histories.

FUTURE WORK · KNOWLEDGE-PRODUCING RESEARCH MEMORY

Keep structured records that link hypotheses, implementation versions, conditions, failures, and replications; evaluate extracted knowledge by whether an independent system can implement an inferred design rule and confirm its predictions on unseen tasks.

Section 8.1 · page 50
05

When does recursive self-improvement become sustained and reinforcing?

Successive improvements to an artifact do not show that the mechanism producing them has become more capable. A recursive claim needs evidence that an earlier improvement strengthens the production of later ones; sustained and accelerating improvement are distinct claims. Can gains in inference, learning, data, and infrastructure reinforce one another after integration and verification costs?

FUTURE WORK · LONGITUDINAL AND CROSS-FAMILY RECURSIVE-IMPROVEMENT EXPERIMENTS

Test each generation on common targets and held-out research tasks under matched budgets, against a frozen initial improver and a history-only control; use selective rollback to check whether later gains causally depend on earlier discoveries.
